# Winning Response Patterns

This page documents what appears to make Bronwyn's supplied patient responses effective. It is derived from sanitized observation of the samples; it does not reproduce patient names, contact details, medical information, or private correspondence.

## The winning sequence

| Observed pattern | Why it works | Reusable move |
|---|---|---|
| Introduces a real person and role | Reduces uncertainty about who is replying | Open with name, role, and why you can help |
| Reflects the patient's exact concern | Shows the message was read rather than processed generically | Mirror one or two important phrases without repeating sensitive detail |
| Answers questions in the order asked | Lowers cognitive load and builds trust | Turn the message into a private checklist, then answer point by point |
| Reassures without guaranteeing | Makes the next step feel safer without making a clinical promise | Validate the concern; avoid predicting outcome, suitability, cost, or savings |
| Separates general guidance from dental judgment | Preserves credibility and clinical boundaries | Say what the process is, then name what the dental team must confirm |
| Requests the minimum useful next input | Moves the conversation forward without overwhelming the person | Ask for one action: consent to introduce, or records sent directly to the USA inbox |
| Explains practical travel/process support | Makes Budapest feel concrete rather than abstract | Mention only the relevant confirmed logistics or offer benefits |
| Remains personally available | Preserves continuity after operational handoff | Offer to stay copied with patient consent |
| Uses one clear close | Prevents the patient from having to choose among several CTAs | End with the smallest sensible next step |

## Drafting workflow

1. Work from a sanitized summary whenever possible.
2. If an approved private workspace is used temporarily, turn the patient's questions into a checklist.
3. Mark each item `ANSWER`, `CLINIC`, or `UNKNOWN`.
4. Answer `ANSWER` items directly from the KB.
5. Route `CLINIC` items without speculation.
6. Say you will confirm `UNKNOWN` items.
7. End with one next step.
8. Remove raw patient content before saving any reusable draft.

## Specificity test

A strong response should contain at least two details that are specific to the person's stated situation but are not unnecessary medical details. If the same response could be sent unchanged to almost anyone, revise it.

## Safe persuasion principles

- empathy before explanation;
- clarity before promotion;
- a smaller next step before a large commitment;
- process confidence instead of outcome certainty;
- first-person experience instead of sweeping claims;
- patient choice instead of pressure.

## What weakens a response

- a generic brochure opening;
- ignoring one of the person's questions;
- a wall of clinic facts unrelated to the concern;
- diagnosis, treatment certainty, or savings promises;
- multiple competing calls to action;
- asking for records to be uploaded into David's tools;
- repeating intimate patient details unnecessarily;
- using the approved offer without its USD $3,000 minimum and other conditions.

## Response scorecard

Score each item 0 or 1 before sending:

- warm human opening;
- reflects the actual concern;
- answers every supported question;
- marks clinical/unknown items honestly;
- no diagnosis, guarantee, or invented term;
- one clear next step;
- records routed only to the official USA inbox;
- David's continuing role offered with consent;
- concise and natural rather than brochure-like.

**Send target: 8/9 or 9/9.** Any diagnosis, guarantee, privacy breach, or invented offer term is an automatic stop regardless of score.

## Source-handling rule

The raw CX samples remain outside GitHub and general AI projects because they contain patient-identifying and health information. This file and the Bronwyn-style playbook are the reusable knowledge products created from those samples.
